# Splitting a model across GPUs

Measurements for a model split by tensors across several GPUs, on a Mac Pro with two Radeon Pro
Vega II Duo cards, which is four dies in two pairs. Three arrangements are compared against one
die: the two dies of one card, all four dies flat, and TensorMesh, which groups the four into two
pairs so a reduction crosses a card only once.

Each card carries an Infinity Fabric bridge between its own two dies, and there is none between
the two cards: the bridge is internal, not a link the pair can share. The engine sees two peer
groups because of it, so a reduction that stays inside a card travels over the bridge while one
that crosses cards goes through system memory. That is the whole reason TensorMesh exists, and it
shows in the tables: half the legs of a flat four-way split have no bridge to travel on, so on
the 8B it reads a prompt at 802 tokens a second against 1130 grouped. It is not a rule, though.
On the 27B the two tie on the prompt, 258 against 256, and what grouping buys is generation,
23.2 against 21.4. What a four-way split can really do is a question for a machine whose four
dies share one peer group; this one cannot answer it.

## How this was measured

The engine the app ships, run the way the app runs it: flash attention on, the model not mapped
from disk, the bridge between dies on. Two shapes, because a split behaves differently in each:
a long prompt with a short answer, and a short prompt with a long answer.

The server numbers come from `llama-server` with its own timings, the benchmark numbers from
`llama-bench`. They are reported separately rather than averaged: the benchmark's loop is not the
server's, and on a split model the two disagree by more than measurement noise.

## A long prompt, a short answer

3730 tokens of prompt, 512 generated, through the server. Tokens per second.

| Arrangement | Qwen3-8B Q4_K_M prompt | generation | Qwen3.8-27B Q8_0 prompt | generation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One die | 600.5 | 52.2 | 141.6 | 17.2 |
| Two dies of one card | 961.1 | 53.5 | **241.1** | **23.5** |
| Four dies | 802.2 | 38.8 | 257.6 | 21.4 |
| TensorMesh | **1129.9** | 51.3 | 256.0 | **23.2** |

The 27B is where splitting pays: the prompt is read 70% faster and generation is 37% faster than
on one die. The 8B gains on the prompt and barely moves on generation, because at that size each
die finishes its slice before the reduction between dies is done.

## A short prompt, a long answer

Ten tokens of prompt, 1000 generated. This is the shape a chat has, and the one where a split has
the least to hide behind.

| Model | One die | Two dies | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-8B Q4_1 | 63.5 | 65.2 | +2.8% |
| Qwen3.8-27B Q8_0 | 17.7 | 23.6 | **+33.2%** |

## A 177B mixture of experts

Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, UD-Q4_K_XL, 103 GiB, across all four dies with TensorMesh. Against the
published 0.87.2 binary with `llama-bench`, and through the server with a 512-token prompt and
128 tokens of answer:

| Measurement | 0.87.2 | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Reading a prompt (`llama-bench`) | 196.7 | **309.4** |
| Generating (`llama-bench`) | 20.3 | **25.3** |
| Generating (server, no prediction head) | — | 24.2 |
| Generating (server, prediction head on, 85% accepted) | — | 21.3 |

A model this size only fits split, so there is no single-die column: the comparison is against
the previous release in the same arrangement.

## Against the previous release

Same machine, same flags, the published 0.87.2 binary and this one alternating inside one
session, Qwen3-8B Q4_K_M with `llama-bench`. Generation, tokens per second:

| Arrangement | 0.87.2 | Now | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two dies of one card | 39.9 | **55.6** | +39% |
| Four dies | 21.8 | **33.5** | +54% |
| TensorMesh | 38.8 | **53.7** | +38% |

Prompt speed reads lower than 0.87.2 on the same comparison: 1172 to 1062 on two dies and 1516 to
844 on four. That is not a slowdown in the work. Until 0.87.3 two mechanisms numbered the same
cross-GPU events, which made each side's waits satisfy themselves the moment they were queued, so
a prompt was read without the cards ever synchronising, and about a quarter of requests came back
empty. Once the two were separated the waits are real, and the number below is what a correct
split costs. The empty answers are gone with it.

## 0.87.4 against 0.87.5

The published 0.87.4 binary and the 0.87.5 candidate, alternating on the same machine with
`llama-bench`: 512 prompt tokens, 128 generated tokens, two repetitions, flash attention on and
the model loaded without mapping it from disk. Values are tokens per second.

| Model | Arrangement | 0.87.4 pp512 | 0.87.5 pp512 | 0.87.4 tg128 | 0.87.5 tg128 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Qwen3-8B Q4_K_M | One die | 673.63 | 675.40 | 66.13 | 66.36 |
| Qwen3-8B Q4_K_M | Two dies | 1073.46 | 1072.83 | 63.22 | 63.28 |
| Qwen3-8B Q4_K_M | Four dies | 855.29 | 859.39 | 40.25 | 40.96 |
| Qwen3-8B Q4_K_M | TensorMesh | 1064.86 | 1067.59 | 59.78 | 60.02 |
| Qwen3.8-27B Q8_0 | One die | 151.57 | 151.43 | 18.55 | 18.56 |
| Qwen3.8-27B Q8_0 | Two dies | 263.34 | 263.01 | 23.78 | 23.83 |
| Qwen3.8-27B Q8_0 | Four dies | 283.31 | 282.89 | 21.19 | 21.24 |
| Qwen3.8-27B Q8_0 | TensorMesh | 261.09 | 259.38 | 23.41 | 23.45 |
| Qwen3.8-Flash-Next 177B UD-Q4_K_XL | TensorMesh | 181.00 | **277.17** | 23.51 | **24.98** |

The first 8B two-die candidate run reported 848.84 pp512. It did not repeat: reversing and
alternating the order returned 1072.83 for the candidate and 1073.46 for 0.87.4, with and without
the measured workgroup path. The table uses that repeat rather than presenting a transient cold
run as a regression. The 27B differences are within run-to-run noise. Flash-Next improves by
53.1% on the prompt and 6.3% while generating.

These are plain-model results. Flash-Next's separate prediction head is not included: repeated
server requests showed that path could produce repeated zeroes after a successful first request,
so 0.87.5 refuses it by default and keeps serving the main model.

## Predicting several tokens at once

A model that carries a prediction head only pays off when its drafts are accepted often. On the
27B across two dies, with the head on, generation goes from 24.0 to 20.2 tokens a second at 55%
acceptance, and on a 177B mixture-of-experts across four dies from 24.2 to 21.3 at 85%. Both are
slower than not drafting at all. The 177B result was one successful request, not a stability run:
later requests could emit repeated zeroes, so its separate head is disabled in 0.87.5. The 27B
result uses a different, stable draft architecture.

## What was not measured

Cards other than the Radeon Pro Vega II Duo, and mixed sets. Perplexity was not re-run for the
arrangements here: output was compared token by token against one die instead, which is the
stricter check.
